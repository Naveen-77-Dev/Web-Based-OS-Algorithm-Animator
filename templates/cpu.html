CPU Scheduling Algorithms

What is CPU Scheduling?

CPU Scheduling is the process of determining which process in the ready queue will be allocated the CPU. The main objective is to maximize CPU utilization and throughput while minimizing response time, waiting time, and turnaround time.

Available Algorithms:

First Come First Serve (FCFS)

The simplest scheduling algorithm that executes processes in order of arrival. While easy to implement, it can lead to the "convoy effect" where short processes wait behind long ones.

Shortest Job First (SJF)

Selects the process with the smallest burst time to execute next. This minimizes average waiting time but may cause starvation of longer processes.

Round Robin (RR)

Each process gets a small unit of CPU time (time quantum). After this time has elapsed, the process is preempted and added to the end of the ready queue.

Priority Scheduling

Assigns priorities to each process, and the process with the highest priority is executed first. Can lead to starvation of low-priority processes.
